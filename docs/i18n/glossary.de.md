## Notes for the native reviewer

- Address: informal "du" throughout; "ihr" only where two players share a screen.
- Pokémon names, places, badges and trainer classes use the official German Gen 1 names: PROF. EICH, ALABASTIA, VERTANIA CITY, MARMORIA CITY, ORANIA CITY, PRISMANIA CITY, FUCHSANIA CITY, SAFFRONIA CITY, ZINNOBERINSEL, MONDBERG, SIEGESSTRASSE, INDIGO PLATEAU, AZURIA-HÖHLE, RUHMESHALLE, LIGA; FELSORDEN etc.; KNIRPS, GÖRE, KÄFERSAMMLER, STREBER, ASS-TRAINER, ARENALEITER, TOP VIER, CHAMP; RATTFRATZ, SAFCON, PUMMELUFF, ARKTOS, LAVADOS, MEWTU, ALTBERNSTEIN. Please check each against the German games.
- Doubt 1: player and rival are ROT and BLAU (German game defaults), and the cartridges GELB/BLAU. The French file kept RED/BLUE. "PokeFan Chess" stays as the title; "Red vs Rocket" became "Rot gegen Rocket".
- Doubt 2: gym leaders and Elite Four use the German names (ROCKO, MAJOR BOB, PYRO, AGATHE, SIEGFRIED); MISTY, ERIKA, KOGA, SABRINA, GIOVANNI, LORELEI, BRUNO unchanged. Original trainers (TOBY, GUS, ROCCO, IRIS...) kept as is.
- Doubt 3: grammar around {piece}, {front}, {back}: these are filled with Pokémon names, so sentences avoid articles or use neuter "dein" ("das Pikachu"). A masculine-feeling name may read oddly; please spot-check in the game.
- "Trainer Card" is "Trainerpass" (game term), "badge" is "Orden"; the piece-style "badge" (the chess symbol on a piece) is "Abzeichen" to keep it apart from gym badges.
- Team Rocket's defeat line uses the German anime catchphrase ("ein Schuss in den Ofen"). Please confirm it lands with children.
- "Level" is used for the Trainer Level (Trainer-Level), "Stufe" for computer difficulty and puzzle steps.
- The disclaimer "Not affiliated or endorsed" is "Nicht verbunden und nicht unterstützt"; a lawyer-ish alternative is "Keine Verbindung zu den Rechteinhabern und nicht von ihnen unterstützt".
